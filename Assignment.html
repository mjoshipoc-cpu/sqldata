Your zip code can predict your health outcomes better than your genetic code. We decided to test that using our own data at EXL. Why we look beyond clinical data When we scored members using only clinical and claims history, most looked fine. But once we added neighborhood data — income, food access, housing — the picture changed for thousands of people. Some looked "low risk" on paper, but their neighborhood told a different story. Clinical data alone can't see that. That's why this series exists. The challenge Health data comes packaged by zip code. But zip codes are mail-delivery boundaries, not neighborhood boundaries — one zip code can cover several very different neighborhoods. When we checked our data, we found 2 out of every 3 zip codes span more than one neighborhood. Match on zip code alone, and you blur different neighborhoods together — and lose the exact signal you're trying to find. How we solved it We built a lookup table connecting each zip code to its exact neighborhoods, so no neighborhood's data gets blended with its neighbors. Then we double-checked every match by hand instead of assuming a clean result meant it worked. Getting to our "Gold" layer We combine a member's clinical history, financial situation, care usage, and neighborhood conditions into one clean record — with a single risk score and the plain-language reasons behind it. That record is what powers everything else in this series: risk modeling, AI-driven outreach, and measuring what actually works.
