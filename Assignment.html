SDOH RISK PLATFORM — 5-MINUTE BUSINESS SCRIPT (Condensed from business_script.txt — read-through pace, ~5 min) ------------------------------------------------ [0:00–0:30] THE PROBLEM ------------------------------------------------ "Every health plan already scores risk from claims and clinical history. But there's a category of member who looks fine on paper — no hospitalizations, no red flags — until you look at where they live. Food insecurity, unstable housing, no transportation, unemployment. Claims-only models miss these people entirely, until they show up in an ER. That risk is real, and today's models never see it." ------------------------------------------------ [0:30–1:30] THE SOLUTION ------------------------------------------------ "We built a platform that combines clinical data with social determinants of health — SDOH — into one risk score, then automatically figures out who needs help, what kind, and drives outreach end-to-end until it's actually resolved. Four things happen: 1. We score every member twice — clinical-only, and clinical-plus-social — so we can measure exactly how much social circumstances change their real risk. 2. We flag the members whose risk is genuinely social-driven, not just clinically severe — the ones a traditional model misses. 3. A 4-agent AI pipeline explains why they're at risk, what they need first, how to reach them, and builds a concrete plan — Agents 1 and 2 explain the 'why' and 'what,' Agents 3 and 4 decide the 'how' based on that foundation, in that order, every time. 4. Outreach goes out automatically and adapts to what the member actually says — but nothing gets marked resolved without a human confirming it really happened. On the model side: the live AI running all four agents today is Groq's Llama 3.3. There's also a Claude Sonnet integration configured in the code as a future option — it's wired up but not switched on yet, worth mentioning if asked which model is actually doing the work." ------------------------------------------------ [1:30–2:15] THE DATA ------------------------------------------------ "Two sources, both free, public, verified straight from Snowflake Marketplace — nothing custom-built. Clinical and claims data comes from Synthea, MITRE's synthetic patient generator — realistic but not real patients, which is exactly why we can use it in a demo. Neighborhood and social data comes from SafeGraph's Open Census Data — real U.S. Census Bureau statistics on poverty, unemployment, food access, housing, down to the ZIP level. So: the social layer is genuinely real; the clinical layer is synthetic but methodologically realistic." ------------------------------------------------ [2:15–3:15] HOW THE SCORING WORKS ------------------------------------------------ "We run two parallel prediction models on the same population — one on clinical and financial data alone, one with the full social layer added. Both sort members into Critical, High, Moderate, Low. Comparing the two for the same member is how we get 'SDOH Lift' — the gap between them — which is the single number that proves the whole point: how much a claims-only model would have missed for that person. One guardrail worth naming: there's a composite neighborhood-disadvantage score that would be tempting to feed the model directly — we deliberately exclude it from training because it's built from the same data as what we're predicting. We only give the model raw underlying facts, not a shortcut answer. And every score comes with an explanation — the top five factors driving it for that specific person, not a generic statement." ------------------------------------------------ [3:15–4:15] TRUST & GUARDRAILS ------------------------------------------------ "Wherever the AI writes language, it's constrained, not left to freelance. It's handed pre-decided facts rather than raw numbers to interpret — if a member isn't high-cost by our own definition, it's told that explicitly and can't describe her otherwise. It's told outright what NOT to speculate about. Some agents never even receive sensitive data like cost or risk score, so they structurally can't misuse it. Wherever a decision doesn't need language — building a plan template, translating a clinical code — we use fixed logic, not AI, so there's zero hallucination risk there by construction. And when the AI genuinely isn't sure what a member meant, it says so and falls back to a safe default instead of guessing confidently. Most important: nothing closes a loop without a human. The AI can flag a possible signal that a referral was completed — a care manager has to type an actual confirmation before it's logged as fact." ------------------------------------------------ [4:15–5:00] CLOSE ------------------------------------------------ "So: claims-only models find the people who are already sick. This finds the people who are about to be — while there's still time to act. The reasoning is real, the personalization is real, the closed-loop tracking is real — and everywhere the AI is doing the talking, there's a guardrail underneath it and a human confirming anything that actually matters." ------------------------------------------------ IF ASKED IN Q&A — ONE-LINERS TO HAVE READY ------------------------------------------------ - Snowflake Cortex?
