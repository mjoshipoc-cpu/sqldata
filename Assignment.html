Hook Your zip code can predict your health outcomes better than your genetic code. We decided to test that with our own data. 1. Why we look at SDOH + Clinical — not clinical alone When we scored our members using only clinical and claims history, most looked fine. But when we added neighborhood data — income, food access, housing, education — the picture changed for thousands of people. Out of 1.2 million member records, 22,370 people (about 2 in every 100) looked low-risk based on their medical history alone — but their neighborhood told a different story. High poverty, food deserts, no reliable transportation. We call this "hidden risk" — real risk that clinical data alone simply cannot see. That's the whole reason this series exists. 2. The challenge Health data and neighborhood data don't naturally speak the same language. CDC health data comes organized by zip code. But neighborhoods (census tracts) are smaller and more precise than zip codes. When we checked, 2 out of every 3 zip codes actually cover more than one neighborhood. If you just match on zip code, you blur many different neighborhoods into one number — and lose the exact signal you're trying to find. 3. How we solved it Instead of a simple zip-code match, we built a proper bridge between zip codes and neighborhoods, so each area's data reflects its own conditions — not an average of several places nearby. We also checked every match for gaps instead of trusting a clean-looking row count. 4. How we get to our "Gold" layer We start with a member's basic profile, then layer in: their clinical history, their financial situation (like unpaid bills or high copay burden), how often they use care (ER visits, missed checkups), and their neighborhood's conditions (income, food access, safety). All of it comes together into one clean, member-level record — with a single risk score, and the top reasons behind that score, in plain terms. That record is what powers everything in the rest of this series: risk modeling, AI-driven outreach, and measuring what actually helps.
